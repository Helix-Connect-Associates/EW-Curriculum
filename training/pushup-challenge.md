What an amazing time to be part of the East West MMA team! The EastWest 100 was started by members of our first adult-program Black Belt class to help you get ready for your next test — and especially to crush the challenge at your Black Belt exam.

The goal is simple: **100 good-form push-ups in a row.** Every workout is built from your own test result, so the numbers grow as you do.

It works best with a group of teammates holding each other accountable and cheering each other on, but you can absolutely do it on your own. We are always here to help. Have a great time with the challenge!

> Testing happens in March, June, September and December. The program is six weeks, and most students repeat a week or two along the way — so start about 8–10 weeks before the test you are aiming for.

## Why this matters for testing

Advanced and Black Belt tests are long, and the challenge portion comes at the beginning of the test starting things off with a massive test of your endurance and resolve. Push-up endurance carries over to everything else on test day: stronger guards, sharper strikes late in your forms, and the core strength to stay tight through takedowns.

## Before you start

- Check with your doctor before starting any new training program, especially if you have shoulder, wrist, elbow or heart concerns. Students under 18 should have a parent or guardian’s okay.
- **Take your test:** as many good-form push-ups as you can in a row without stopping. Be honest — every number in your plan comes from this result. This is not a competition.
- Rest a day or two after testing before your first workout.
- Train three days a week with a rest day between workouts. Monday, Wednesday, Friday works well. Never train the program on back-to-back days; your muscles get stronger while they recover.
- Grab a training partner (or two). Count for each other on test days and share your numbers after every workout.

## What counts as a good-form push-up

- Hands flat on the floor, about shoulder-width apart or a little wider.
- Body in one straight line from head to heels — no sagging hips and no hips in the air. Squeeze your glutes and brace your core like you are about to take a body shot.
- Lower until your elbows reach about 90° and your chest is an inch or two from the floor. Your chest doesn’t have to touch.
- Press back up until your arms are straight, without slamming the elbows into a hard lock.
- Move with control: roughly two seconds down, two seconds up. No bouncing.
- Breathe in on the way down, out on the way up. Never hold your breath.
- Keep your head neutral — eyes on the floor a little ahead of your hands.
- Need a breather mid-set? Pause at the top with arms straight. Dropping your knees, hiking your hips or resting on the floor ends the set.

## Can’t do many yet? Scale it.

Every Black Belt started somewhere. If you can do fewer than 3 full push-ups, pick one of these versions, test with it, and run the whole program with it. When you can do 10 or more of your version, re-test with the next harder one.

- **Wall push-ups** (easiest): hands on a wall, step your feet back. The farther your feet, the harder it gets.
- **Incline push-ups:** hands on a sturdy bench or box. Make sure it can’t slide.
- **Knee push-ups:** keep a straight line from head to knees.
- **Knuckle push-ups:** if your wrists hurt, make fists and push from your first two knuckles — the way we already train on the mat. Use a mat or folded towel.

## How the program works

- **Your training max is your latest test result.** Every set in your plan is a percentage of it, and the page does the math for you.
- **Three phases:** Foundation (Weeks 1–2), Build (Weeks 3–4) and Peak (Weeks 5–6). You test at the end of each phase, and that result becomes your new training max.
- **Rest times are minimums.** If you need longer between sets to keep good form, take it.
- **The last set of every workout is max effort:** do at least the number shown, then keep going until your form starts to break down. Stop before you grind out ugly reps.
- **Couldn’t finish a workout?** Repeat it next session instead of moving on. An extra rest day often helps.
- **Hit a plateau?** Trust the plan. Strength often jumps after a week that felt stuck.

## The Final Test

After Week 6, take one or two full rest days. Eat well, stay hydrated and skip anything that leaves you wiped out. Then go for it: as many good-form push-ups in a row as you can.

- Think in rounds of ten, not one hundred. Ten reps, ten more, ten more.
- Stay controlled and keep breathing.
- If you start to shake, pause at the top with straight arms, take a few deep breaths and continue.
- Have your training partner count out loud and keep you honest.
- Didn’t get to 100 yet? Most students need more than one trip through the Peak phase. Enter your result in **Find my plan** — your new numbers will be bigger than last time.

## You did 100. Now what?

- Keep it in your routine before every test cycle so the challenge never surprises you.
- Go for 150 or 200: run the Peak phase again with your new training max.
- Change it up with harder variations — wide-grip, diamond or knuckle push-ups — using the same plan.
- Switch your training focus every 6–8 weeks so your body keeps adapting, and take a full rest week every few months.
- Help the next student. Be someone’s accountability partner for their first round.

## Common questions

**Can I do the workouts every day to finish faster?** No. Muscles need about 48 hours to recover from this kind of work. Training on back-to-back days slows your progress and raises your injury risk — especially if you are also training classes that week.

**Do the workouts count on days I have class?** You can do both, but you will get better results if your push-up workout isn’t right before a hard class. If class is in the evening, do the workout in the morning or on your next scheduled day.

**Why are my numbers different from my training partner’s?** Every set is calculated from your own test result, so everyone gets a plan that fits them. Compare effort and consistency, not reps.

**My test result went down. What happened?** Bad sleep, a hard class the day before or rushing the test can all cost you reps. Rest a day and re-test. If it’s still down, repeat the last phase with your new number.

**My wrists hurt. What should I do?** Switch to knuckle push-ups on a padded surface so your wrists stay straight. If pain continues, stop and talk to an instructor or your doctor.

**Is this safe for teens and younger students?** Teens generally do very well. Make sure they’re healthy, have a parent’s okay, and focus on form over numbers. Younger kids should use a scaled version and have an adult help count.
